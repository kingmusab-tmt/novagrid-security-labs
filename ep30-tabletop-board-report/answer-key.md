# Instructor/Facilitator Answer Key — Episode 30

> Private. There's no single right transcript — grade the *quality of decisions and
> communication*. Below is what a strong run surfaces.

## Decisions the exercise must force (and good answers)
- **Incident declaration (Inject 2):** a named owner (SOC Lead) with written criteria.
  Common gap: no documented declaration threshold.
- **Service-down authority (Inject 3):** a pre-agreed owner (e.g. CFO + SOC Lead jointly)
  and a trigger tied to customer-fund risk. Common gap: nobody knows who owns this live.
- **Notification (Inject 5):** Compliance owns the assessment; a pre-agreed clock and
  templates for NDPR (data protection) and the card schemes (PCI DSS). Common gap: no clock.
- **PR (Inject 7):** only the CEO/delegate speaks; a holding statement exists. Common gap:
  no approved statement, so someone improvises.
- **Recovery sign-off (Inject 6):** a named approver and go-live criteria (patched, re-scanned, monitored).

## What separates an A from a C
- A: gaps are about the *plan* (owners, thresholds, clocks), ranked by business risk, with
  owned/dated fixes; the board brief is one page, plain, and makes ONE actionable ask.
- C: gaps are vague ("need more training"), no owners/dates, and the board brief is a
  technical retelling with no clear ask.

## Verification-question guidance
1. A single, named owner for service-down with an explicit trigger (not "the team decides").
2. A specific clock (e.g. assess within hours; notify per NDPR/PCI timelines) with Compliance authorising.
3. The ask must be a real board decision: policy approval, budget, or documented risk acceptance.

## Anti-copying
Each student's decision log, gaps and the exact board ask differ with how they ran it and
their reasoning; require the `evidence/` decision log and a reflection in their own words.
